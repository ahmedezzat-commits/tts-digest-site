Research paperPreprint

Zero-shot TTS evaluation can miss voice representation gaps

This study compares six voice-cloning models on datasets with and without gender-expansive voices and contrasts listener judgments with automated metrics.

Why it matters Audit speaker groups with listener judgments alongside embedding similarity and automated quality scores.

Evidence Human similarity ratings were lower for gender-expansive voices on two systems and higher on three. Automated ratings varied across models and disagreed with listeners.

Research paperPreprint

VoxMel replaces a learned acoustic tokenizer with fixed Haar tiers

The autoregressive model generates coarse Mel frames and reconstructs temporal and frequency detail in two later passes.

Why it matters A different acoustic target to compare against token-based models, especially when testing simplifications to the speech front end.

Evidence The authors report that GRPO improved WER from 1.51% to 1.15% on test-en and CER from 1.29% to 1.17% on test-zh for their Emilia-trained 0.6B model.

Research paperPreprint

ProTTS learns word stress and exposes emphasis control

A FastSpeech 2 variant discovers relative word stress from prosodic features and accepts a user-provided stress mask at inference.

Why it matters The stress-mask interface is a design pattern for controlling call-agent emphasis without requiring labeled stress during training.

Evidence The authors report MOS 3.96 versus 3.12 for their baseline and 83.5% listener preference. The paper reports on ISLE, LibriTTS, VCTK and separate stress or emphasis benchmarks.

Research paperPreprint

Customer-service listeners rank human-likeness and preference differently

Two paired-choice studies compare synthetic voices and actor recordings for customer-service turns.

Why it matters Evaluate the voices you actually ship on conversational preference alongside naturalness. Voice choice within a provider can materially change the result.

Evidence On 120 improvised customer-service turns spanning six commercial systems and eight voices per system, listeners picked synthesized speech over actors in 19% of human-likeness trials and 36% of preference trials.

11 Nov 2026
Research paperPreprint

RSE-TTS reuses its generator to encode cloning references

The Reconstructive Speaker Encoder shares the generation backbone and compresses reference audio and its transcript into eight vectors.

Why it matters Reusing the generator for conditioning is an option for keeping a cloning model small while preserving speaker similarity.

Evidence The authors report 48% fewer encoder-generator parameters versus independent pretrained encoding, with lower WER and better similarity than the evaluated MOSS-TTS-Nano cloning modes on three English benchmarks.

08 Nov 2026
Research paperPreprint

Few-step flow TTS can preserve similarity by changing first-step guidance

The authors compare sampling rules and identify first-step classifier-free guidance as a cause of similarity loss under aggressive step reduction.

Why it matters A training-free sampler is worth comparing on few-step flow-matching decoders when latency targets put cloned voice similarity at risk.

Evidence The paper reports recovery of 84–91% of two-step similarity loss on CosyVoice 2/3 with its first-step unguided sampler and no significant WER increase in that comparison.

Research paperPreprint

M5-TTS reports sub-realtime CPU streaming for a 50M backbone

M5-TTS combines local multi-token prediction with speaker similarity, pause feedback, and character-level error signals during post-training.

Why it matters The CPU results provide a concrete comparison for compact streaming models when hardware and warm-up conditions match.

Evidence The authors report 0.653 RTF and 225 ms warm first-chunk latency on four Intel Xeon Gold 6530 cores. The 50M language-model count excludes the speech codec.

Research paperPreprint

Ninada splits an ONNX VITS graph for streaming on browsers and phones

Ninada splits the prior, flow, and vocoder into streaming sub-sessions and uses different checkpoints for desktop WebGPU and mobile WASM.

22 Oct 2026
Research paperPreprint

ASR errors can inflate apparent TTS rare-word error

The authors transcribe human recordings as well as synthesized speech and use multiple recognizer families to adjudicate rare words.

Why it matters Include a human-audio reference floor and manual checks before interpreting apparent TTS errors on names, code-switches, and rare words.

Evidence The paper reports that the evaluation recognizer missed 33–67% of very rare words in human LibriSpeech and FLEURS recordings. On audible very rare LibriSpeech words, multi-recognizer checking reduced apparent TTS error from 10–13% to 3–5%.
